## Quick Reference Thresholds
  - R² > 0.5: acceptable model explanation of variance.
  - Q² > 0.3: acceptable predictive power.
  - High R² with low Q²: potential overfitting.
  - Hotelling’s T²: identifies overall outliers in score space.
  - DModX: identifies moderate outliers based on residual variance.
  - Scores plot: close clusters indicate similarity, distant clusters indicate difference.
  - Loading plot: high-loading variables are main drivers of variance.

## PLS Regression Interpretation Rules
  - Regression coefficients indicate the direction and magnitude of each variable’s effect on the response.
  - Positive coefficients mean the variable increases the predicted response; negative coefficients mean it decreases the response.
  - Variable Importance in Projection (VIP) scores above 1 indicate variables with strong influence on the model.
  - Coefficients with low magnitude and VIP below 0.5 are considered weak or unimportant.
  - Always cross-check regression weights with loading plots to confirm consistency.
  - High multicollinearity among predictors can distort regression weights; interpret with caution.
  - For predictive use, ensure Q² is sufficiently high to avoid unreliable extrapolation.

## VIP Plot Interpretation Rules
  - VIP scores show the relative importance of each variable in explaining the model.
  - If the M1.VIP data is positive, the variable has a positive correlation with the output.
  - If the M1.VIP data is negative, the variable has a negative correlation with the output.
  - The higher the magnitude of the VIP score, the stronger the correlation.
  - VIP > 1: strong contributor to the model.
  - VIP between 0.5–1: moderately important.
  - VIP < 0.5: weak or unimportant variable.
  - Always compare VIP results with regression coefficients and loading plots to confirm interpretation.
